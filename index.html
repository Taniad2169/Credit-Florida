Rules & Resources for Selling Credit Repair

General information, not legal advice. Laws change: confirm every item with a licensed attorney in each state where you do business before you sell credit repair services.

Before you sign your first client

  1. Register in your state and post any bond it requires before advertising or selling. Many states require registration plus a surety bond (Florida, for example, requires registration with FDACS and a bond).
  2. Written contract signed by the client, with the full services, total cost, estimated time to results, and your business name and address (CROA, 15 U.S.C. 1679d).
  3. Give the federal disclosure statement "Consumer Credit File Rights Under State and Federal Law" (15 U.S.C. 1679c) on a separate page, before the contract is signed, and keep a signed copy for 2 years.
  4. 3-business-day right to cancel, with the cancellation form attached to the contract (15 U.S.C. 1679e).
  5. No payment before the service is fully performed (CROA 1679b(b)). If you sell by phone, the Telemarketing Sales Rule also bans fees until results are shown with a credit report issued more than 6 months after the results were achieved.
  6. No guarantees, no misleading claims: never promise a score increase or the removal of accurate, timely information, and never advise disputing accurate information as wrong or creating a new identity (CPN / file segregation).
  7. Written authorization before any credit pull, and only for the client's own file (FCRA permissible purpose).
  8. Information security program under the FTC Safeguards Rule: a person in charge, risk assessment, multi-factor sign-in, staff training, vendor oversight, and FTC notice within 30 days of a breach affecting 500 or more consumers.
  9. Privacy notice to clients explaining what you collect and share (GLBA Privacy Rule), and E-SIGN consent before using electronic signatures.
  10. Telemarketing: check the National Do Not Call Registry before calling, and follow your state's telemarketing rules.

Legal & Government Information

Important Disclosures & Legal Information:

  • Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA): You have the right to dispute inaccurate information on your credit report. Credit bureaus must investigate disputes within 30 days.
  • Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA): Debt collectors cannot harass you, use false statements, or engage in unfair practices when collecting debts.
  • Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA): Credit repair companies cannot make false claims, charge fees before services are rendered, or guarantee specific results.
  • Free Annual Credit Reports: You are entitled to one free credit report every 12 months from each of the three major credit bureaus through AnnualCreditReport.com.
  • Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB): The CFPB provides resources and accepts complaints about credit reporting issues and credit repair services.
  • Federal Trade Commission (FTC): The FTC enforces consumer protection laws and provides information about credit repair rights and scams to avoid.
